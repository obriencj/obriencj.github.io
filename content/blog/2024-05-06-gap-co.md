Early on this year I had decided to throw myself into a seven day ride
from Pittsburgh, PA to Washington, DC. 330 miles all on my lonesome,
just me and the bike and whatever I brought on it. Here is the
chronicle of the events of that adventure.

<!-- more -->


# 2024-04-22, 4 Days Prior

Panic is setting in. I need to redo the wiring on the Rove. I need to
get the wheels checked to make sure my spokes and rims are in good
tune. I need to re-align my brake calipers a bit. I've made a list of
what I'm packing, but I haven't actually tried to make it fit in the
panniers yet.

I've got a week off of work, and initial planning had put most of the
tasks I needed done in this week or after the trip. Of course that
didn't last, and now work is slipping and landing right when I'll be
gone, which means I need to arrange for coverage.

I've been doing some long rides to get my body back up to spec, but
I'm still not back to my fitness levels from the end of 2023. February
really set me back.


## Later that Evening

I spent a bit of time working to re-route the wiring so it wasn't
relying on so much electrician tape. In so doing, I managed to split
the shift cable housing, which was quite brittle, by the rear
derailleur. In a panic I tried to find a newer cable and housing, but
had nothing suitable in my spares bin.

Oak City should have some, I'll need to visit them tomorrow so I can
get my shifting reconnected tomorrow.


# 2024-04-23, 3 Days Prior

Was stressed all morning.  I had spent a little more time packing, but
I think I'm over doing it and trying to bring too much stuff.

Called Oak City when they opened, and of course they had what I
needed. Picked it up and had lunch downtown. It was a fight, but I
replaced the cabling, got the wiring patched in, and all seems well
again.


# 2024-04-24, 2 Days Prior

Got the pannier mount locations dialed in, and I added some tape as
padding to keep it from rattling around in an annoying manner. I put
my snacks bag back on the handlebars.

Printed out a copy of train tickets and itinerary. Created the daily
routes on Strava and sync'd them to my Garmin.


# 2024-04-25, 1 Day Prior

Frantically trying to get enough work done so people can cover for me
while I'm on PTO.

All of my devices are on their chargers. Need to double check that I
have the relevant cables along with me.

I need to re-pack. I'm going to take a short ride around the local
loop here fully loaded to validate that everything is stable and feels
alright.

I'll need to leave the house by 8am tomorrow morning to make my
train. Feeling the giddy type of nervous, ready to go but made to wait.


# 2024-04-26, Train Ride from Raleigh

Parked downtown, loaded bags on bike and pedaled the two blocks to the
station. Was on the platform as the train pulled up. Detached my bags,
handed my bike over to a really nice guy who loaded it up. There was
some delay as they needed to clean my room, so I ended up in the
dining car chatting with a fellow who was a frequent rider.

My trip is two train rides to get to the start of the trail. One train
to DC and then another to Pittsburg. As scheduled I should have a
little over an hour to swap trains. The train status page currently
says I should be arriving in DC thirty minutes early, that's wonderful.


## Rocky Mount

Rolled into Rocky Mount at 10:20am. We're supposed to have left at
10:23am, but it's currently 10:36am. Anxiety climbing.

Train dpearted late, at 10:40am. Status page still thinks we'll be
early to DC. We're just creeping slowly along, likely waiting for a
freight train to get off of a shared segment.


## Petersburg

Rolled into Petersburg at 12:26pm. Rolled back out at 12:28pm, nice! I
wish all the stops were this rapid. Maybe we can get some time back
after all.


## Richmond

Rolled into Richmond at 1:00pm. Rolled back out at 1:10, which was
less late than it was supposed to be, so that's good!


## Alexandria

Rolled into Alexandria at 2:50pm. Next stop is mine, so I'm packing up
now so I can be ready to leap off the train and transfer over to the
next one.


## Washington, DC

I heard the calls for "next stop" and everything seemed like I'd have
almost an hour of time to get on my next train. Then we came to a
stop, well short of the station. The conductor announced something
over the intercoms about "waiting on commuter traffic." For thirty
minutes we sat stopped in darkness! Then finally we creaked our way
forward, went underground, and arrived at the station.

It was 3:35! I disembarked, grabbed my bike from the luggage car and
ran with it up to the station. I waited in line at the ticket counter
as minutes passed and then finally got the check tag for my bike. I
hauled ass to platform D, track 15, then all the way to the end for
the baggage car. I handed the bike up to the conductor, then jumped
inside the car. I sat down in my roomette at 3:56. The train rolled
out on time, 4:05. That was cutting it way too close.

I'm safely on the way to Pittsburgh. I don't care if there are stops
along the way, or if we are delayed, just so long as we get there. The
current estimate is 11:50pm. The hotel is expecting me to check in
after midnight already. It's about a 1 mile ride, estimated at 10
minutes.


## Pittsburg, PA

I slept through most of the ride from DC. It was dark and things felt
surreal as I got off of the train and retrieved the bike. I loaded up
my bags and started pedaling. People were out on the street, loud and
happy in front of bars and restaurants. I found my way across the
bridges, and then around the block to the Priory. At first they wanted
me to lock my bike up outside. When I asked if they would simply let
me bring it along into my room they seemed surprised but said that
would be fine.


# 2024-04-27, Riding Day 1

Pittsburg, PA to Connellsville, PA

they day started off with a thin drizzle. It was slightly chilly, but
thankfully not cold. I took most of my breakfast and packed it to
bring along, eating just a ham croisant. Then I carried my bike out of
the hotel, and set off on the streets of Pittsburgh. I eventually
found my way around to the park and that is when I truly began my
Great Allegheny Passage experience. It was paved at first, and went
around underpasses and by abandoned buildings. Crossed a few bridges
and wove through more vacancies. It wasn't until around 21 miles that
the pavement fell away and the gravel began.

It was good condition gravel. Someone had clearly been actively
maintaining this thing, and really cared.

The trail passed through small towns. In many cases right through the
front yards of one row of houses and the back yards of others. I
learned later that the rail line used to be right here, carrying coal
workers to their mine and back home again.

The natural scenery wasn't anything particularly outstanding, it felt
like riding the Neuse River Trail, or the American Tobacco Trail. Just
much, much flatter.

I stopped for water refills a few times, and then at a hot dog stand
for a dog, some fries, and a watermelon sno cone.

I saw a number of other riders, some with more gear than I was
carrying, some with less. All ages out riding. I saw a number of
volunteers as well. Apparently the trail is entirely volunteer
maintained, and they really get into it. That's why the gravel was in
such wonderful condition. I've genuinely been on pavement that wasn't
as consistent and level as this trail was.

Eventually I pulled into Connellsville, and through their downtown I
navigated to the Connellsville B&B. What a lovely place! The hostess
was very kind, showed my to my room (which was wonderful). It also had
a coin operated washing machine, so I immediately showered and put my
riding gear in for a wash. I got dinner at a sandwich shop two blocks
down the road and made it back just before the dryer had finished. I
passed out briefly, woke long enough to respond to a text message with
an emoji, and then fell asleep again around 7:30.

Breakfast the next morning was of course perfect. I filled my bottles
with ice and water, took a complimentary banana, and set off again.


# 2024-04-28, Riding Day 2

Connellsville, PA to Confluence, PA

While the GAP from Pittsburgh to Connellsville hadn't been
particularly scenic (to me, anyway), the ride from Connellsville to
Confluence was beautiful. Gorges and streams, rapids, exposed coal
veins. All the while on perfectly maintained and extremely level
gravel. I stopped frequently to snap photos, and lingered on a very
high bridge looking over Ohiopyle to watch rafters bounce across the
rapids far below me.

I made it to Confluence, crossed the bridge to the peninsula where I
was staying. I was early enough to grab a shower and head to the
corner where a restaurant was open for its first weekend. I had lunch
there and then headed back to the Smith House where I was staying.

I went through my pack to review the things I'd brought along. So much
was geared towards the expectation of colder weather, which just
wasn't the case. I figured out two bags worth of clothes and a jacket
that I could do without, easily 5lbs of clothes and a reasonable
amount of bulk. The post office is only a few blocks away, an easy
walk. In the morning I will have breakfast then take these and mail
them to myself. It'll be nice to have less to carry along with me.

I had dinner at the same restaurant. I will need the calories for
tomorrow, finishing my climb up the continental divide. Once I clear
that, a little over halfway through the day's ride, then it will be
downhill all the way back to DC.

Passed out just after 9pm, a little later than I should really have
stayed up.


# 2024-04-29, Riding Day 3

Confluence, PA to Cumberland, PA

Had coffee on the porch, watched the sun rise and melt away the
morning mist. Confluence is a very quiet town. Had breakfast at 8am
with a few others who were staying at the Inn.

Mailed the 5lbs of extra clothes back to myself, made it back to my
room, brushed my teeth, checked the charges of various devices, packed
up, and set out.

The ride to the continental divide was slightly more of a slog, a bit
more of an uphill grade than previous days. Still nothing serious, but
tiring after many miles. Then I passed through the tunnel at the
continental divide and all at once the climbing was over. From that
point onwards my days would be downhill jaunts instead of slow
inclines.

I made it from Frostberg to Cumberland in fantastic time, just flying
down the gravel. I doubt the strava records for various distances

Checked in to the Fairview, wandered around a bit to find some food.
Started a load of laundry (the previous place hadn't had washing
machines for me to use).

I'm sitting now in the lobby waiting on the dryer. I'm anxious to get
my things sorted out and pass out.

Tomorrow morning I'd like to buy an inexpensive battery-powered light
for the bike. My dynamo is great, but only when I'm moving.


# 2024-04-30, Riding Day 4

Cumberland, PA to Little Orleans, MD

Hotels are weird. They present themselves as so fashionable and
comfortable, but then the room has a fan that sounds like a jet engine
spinning up, and there's flashing LEDs in the smoke detector that
light up the ceiling every few seconds all night long. There's a mini
fridge sure, but then it rattles and whines like it's about to explode
whenever the compressor is on.

At least the continental breakfasts are pretty safe and reliable.

I'm waiting for 9am, so I can buy that extra light. Then I'll pack up
and head on out.

The other places I stayed had ice and water available for me to fill
up my bottles with before I rolled out. This expensive place has
bottles of water that I can purchase.

Got my light, and also bought a soft flask with a water filter
attached. I've been told that most of the water along the way to DC is
from wells, so you need to filter it to make it potable. I'm loaded up
with icewater from the tap here.

Covered in sunscreen, everything's charged up, just need to shove
clothes into saddlebags.

Landed in Little Orleans. Bill's Place was closed. No water to be
had. A guy wandered over, looking forlorn. He had lost his keys, and
was waiting on an uber driver to bring a spare pair. He offered a
bottle of water, for which I was very thankful. I waited out the hour
there, once his uber arrived and got him a key. I'm sure he was very
relieved.

The shuttle eventually arrived, and we stopped at a gas station diner.
I ate there while the driver went back down to get three more riders
that had landed just after me. I was done by the time they arrived.
They took their orders to-go and back into the shuttle we
went. Finally we arrived at the inn. Then we got charged an extra $191
for the shuttle service! No coffee until 7am, breakfast at 8am, then
right onto the shuttle. I'd better pack as soon as I wake up...


# 2024-05-01, Riding Day 5

Little Orleans, MD to Williamsport, MD

Spoke with the other guests over breakfast. There were three brothers
and a fellow named Kingsley. The brothers were not related by blood,
rather two of them had married sisters of the third. They were doing
the C&O on eBikes. Kingsley had a gravel setup very similar to mine.

Shortly after breakfast we were loaded up on to the transport truck
and dropped off at the trailhead. The brothers planned to take the
paved route that ran parallel to the C&O. They lingered a bit, but I
took off as soon as I had my gear sorted. Happy to put Little Orleans
behind me.

The ride to Williamsport itself was uneventful. The locks and lock
houses were in better and better condition. I stopped in Hancock for
lunch, though all I could find was a Burger King. I made sure to get a
picture at lock 45 and mile marker 100.

I was met by another transit truck, and taken a short distance up into
town. I learned that I was staying at the remnants of the farm that
had once owned the entire area, all the way to the river. But as years
had passed it was sold off segment by segment. Now the farmhouse was
the B&B that I would be staying at.

I was the only guest, so I had the run of the place. There were two
dogs living in a large sunroom off of the kitchen. Alpacas grazed in a
field outside. The basement had a pool table, so I spent some of my
time shooting against myself.


# 2024-05-02, Riding Day 6

Williamsport, MD to Brunswick, MD

Back on the trail. I was stopped to take some pics of a water wheel
and the river when I encountered Kingsley again. We chatted for a bit,
and I suggested we ride together for a bit. And so on the route to
Brunswick I had company. We still stopped for photos, and thanks to
him I have photos of myself on this ride that aren't selfies. It was
also his suggestion to take a brief detour and cross the bridge into
West Virginia long enough to get lunch and a beer at the Bavarian Inn.

Brunswick was a mess, and following the route I'd programmed into my
Garmin led us up an absolutely insanely steep half mile stretch of
road. We ended up walking half of it, it was simply too steep. Then in
Brunswick we checked in to the Travellodge. We had to stowe our bikes
in a conference room, they wouldn't allow them into the rooms. I found
that to be pretty odd. There was an attached diner, and that served up
a pretty reasonable dinner.


# 2024-05-03, Riding Day 7

Brunswick, MD to Washington, DC

Met Kingsley once again over breakfast at the diner. We retrieved our
bikes and set off together again. Leaving Brunswick was a lot easier
than entering it.

Kingsley was kind enough to snap a shot of me at mile marker 45.

The river was absolutely beautiful along this segment. It was broad
and rocky and full of rapids.

The gravel path got strangely worse and worse for the wear as we got
closer to DC.

At one point I came to a stop to take a picture, as I'd done many
times before, but I didn't call out that I was stopping loudly
enough. I wish I'd shouted it, because shortly after I'd come to a
full stop I heard skidding and Kingsley hit me at speed. It broke one
of the attachment clasps on my bags and put a gash on his shin. I felt
terrible, I should have been signifigantly louder. He bled a bit, and
thankfully he had some zipties to loan me so I can resecure my bag to
the rack. We continued on.

More and more activity built up on the path. We encountered more
riders, their full jersey regalia and expensive carbon framed bikes
with no baggage declared them as locals out for exercise rather than
travelers.

And then the trail just disappeared into asphalt.

Thankfully Kingsley knew where the mile marker 0 was, past warfside
bars and restaurants, hidden around behind a boathouse. It was unkempt,
but this was it. The end of the trail.

Kingsley was kind enough to point me in the general direction of Q
street, where I was staying. He headed off on his own route home, he
lived in DC.

I passed monuments and commuter cyclists. I got a bit turned around,
but eventually I was on the right streets. The DC cycling
infrastructure is wonderful, with many fully separated bike
paths. There was so much pedestrian traffic.

I eventually found myself in front of the Hotel Cambria. It was very
swank, and I was very tired and dirty. The hotel staff didn't give my
bike a second glance, I checked in and they let me take it up the
elevator and into my delightfully modern room. I showered, I changed,
I put on normal clothing. I went downstairs and had a very nice dinner
at the attached restaurant. Then I returned to my room and slept.


# 2024-05-04, Capitol

Chilling out in DC for the day

Breakfast at the hotel restaurant, sitting at the same spot at the bar
dinner the night prior.

The rain returned. I am so thankful that I managed the entire ride
with only a bit of drizzle in Pittsburg, I was perfectly content to
walk in the rain today. I wandered up to the mall.

I've never been in DC before. The monuments are impressive, and
significantly larger than I realized. There were ducks in the
reflecting pool, and tiny fledgling ducks paddled alongside me as I
walked. I think I enjoyed the ducks more than the rest of it, to be
honest. The big stone things were seen by everyone, I could find
better pictures than any I would be taking that day. But those
ducklings were only ducklings for the moment, only swimming in the
pool for a short window of time. There would be different ducks, but
not those ones. They were just visiting, like me.

I wandered around the warf after having seen my fill of big stone
things. There was an arcade bar, but it was remarkably lame.


# 2024-05-05, Homeward

Train ride home

I checked out of the hotel with plenty of time to kill before my train
ride. I rode slowly to the train station, took some minor detours just
to pad out time. Because my train ticket was for a roomette I was
granted access to the lounge. In particular this was good because I
was able to tag and stowe my bike and bags there, and then go wander
around on foot.

The station essentially had a small mall attached to it. There were
shops, and a food court. I snagged lunch for myself, and then picked
up some fancy chocolate for Nan and for Zoe. I exhausted the
interesting options in short order, so returned to the lounge and was
very startled to find my bike not where I'd left it. I quickly went to
the lounge desk, "the bike that was in there, where did it go??" The
woman at the desk look confused for a moment, and then she said simply
"oh we moved it, we needed space." They'd put it in an adjacent
conference room. I realized that I had been fully in a panic only when
I started to calm down again.

I took my laptop from the bags and sat in a quiet corner of the lounge
until it was finally time for my train. I loaded up, and walked the
bike down to the platform. Removed the bags, handed the bike up to the
fellow in the baggage train, and boarded.

It was a quiet ride back. I slept through most of it. Eventually a
knock at my door came and I was told that the next stop would be
mine. I shook myself awake, and when the train next stopped I
disembarked. Fetched my bike from the baggage car, loaded its bags
back on, walked my way out of the station. It was a short ride across
the familiar streets, up into the parking deck of the Dillon. Around
and around, and there was my car. I unlocked it, loaded my gear
inside, put the bike up onto the rear rack. The drive home felt alien,
surreal. I got home, walked inside with all of my bags, closed the
garage door, and realized that it was well and truly over.

I did it. It was over. I slept in my own bed, and woke up back in my
old life again.
